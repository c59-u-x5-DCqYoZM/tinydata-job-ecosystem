# Anthropic: "Scenarios for Our Economic Future" (September 2026)

- **Page:** https://www.anthropic.com/institute/econ-scenarios (Version 1.0, September 2026). Shared by Soren on 5 October 2026 for the job ecosystem.
- **Technical report:** "Economic Scenarios for Transformative AI", Korinek et al., 2026, https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/files/4zrzovbb/website/cf58f84d46a4a76bf5a5b039ac695fba6b80041c.pdf. Externally reviewed by economists including Daron Acemoglu and David Autor (not asked to endorse the conclusions).

Read through a web fetch summary, not the full report; figures to be checked against the PDF before any external use.

## What it says

- **The model.** An economy is a bundle of tasks. AI can leave a task unchanged, augment it (help a person do it better or faster), automate it, or create new tasks.
- **Three US scenarios to 2030:**

| Scenario | GDP effect | What it assumes | Labour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Modest | +1.6% ($34.1T) | About the impact of the internet | Unemployment and wages historically normal |
| Substantial | +8.3% ($36.3T) | "AI is capable of doing half of all knowledge work by 2030" | Knowledge-worker wages flat, others gain, unemployment around 5%; 59.7% of knowledge workers "still there" by 2030 |
| Extreme | +32.4% ($44.4T) | Recursively self-improving AI; about 15% annual growth | Knowledge-worker wages fall 10%+; unemployment at historic levels; labour share falls from 60 cents to 45.2 cents per dollar |

- **The explorer's five inputs:**
  - **Capabilities:** what tasks AI can do, 0 to 100%
  - **Adoption:** how much people use AI, 0 to 100%
  - **Autonomy:** almost none to almost all
  - **Productivity:** same to 10x
  - **Adjustment:** how long it takes to find a new job, from 1 to 2 months to 3+ years or never
- **Evidence:** a survey of more than 10,000 Americans (August 2026). Site visitors' predictions (30,710) cluster near the substantial scenario.
- **Out of scope by design:** policy responses, business cycles, demand or financial shocks, catastrophic risks.

## Why it matters to TinyData (Claude's reading, not ratified)

1. **Our three sectors are knowledge work.** Professional & Business Services, Technology & Digital and Finance & Insurance sit in the most exposed group, so the job ecosystem should model AI's effect on them rather than assume a static market.
2. **The task model is the right unit for jobs.** Representing each role as a bundle of tasks, each tagged unchanged, augmented, automated or new, lets the ecosystem ask which roles shrink, which change, which appear, and what people in them need next.
3. **The scenarios become a layer in the experiment harness.** Run the same population under modest, substantial and extreme settings, using the explorer's five inputs as levers. The adjustment input is our core metric too: the time it takes a person to find the right next role.
4. **Displacement raises the value of a verified signal.** More moves and more career changers mean more volume, and a greater need to show what a person can actually do rather than a job title that may no longer exist. This is a strong investor argument, to be stated carefully.
5. **The method is a model for ours.** A survey for priors, plus an interactive explorer people can push on, maps onto our consumer research and the financial-modelling interface.

## Caveats

- **US data, UK market.** The figures are US. The UK equivalents need sourcing (for example DfE's work on occupational AI exposure, ONS), and the labour-data research track has been asked for them.
- **Scenarios, not forecasts.** They are scenarios, and the page says so.
